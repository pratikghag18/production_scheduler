## Expected

R-336 (added by the tester in this run, `stated_by: agent`): a stage card's
`refs` name the sessions that shipped it, and a session's `shipped` names the
stages it shipped; the two are one fact written from two ends and must agree.

`6086c23`'s own commit message states the standard it is being held to:

> Eight stages, S26 to S33, grouped from the requirements' own claims **and the
> sessions that shipped them**

and its finding, F-093, says why the link is the deliverable rather than
decoration:

> `shipped` on a session is not decoration, it is the only link between a day's
> work and the shape it belongs to, and an empty one should be as uncomfortable
> to leave as an empty `numbers`.

## Lead

A suspicion, not a fact.

The pattern in the seven broken links is that every wrong session number is in
the 49-52 band and every right one is in the 53-68 band. The nine sessions the
commit added `shipped` to are 53, 54, 59, 60, 61, 62, 63, 66 and 68 — all of
them 4 or 5 September, all of them near the top of the file. The four cards
whose refs are wrong are the four covering work from 2 and 3 September, which is
lower down. So the reading that fits is that the `shipped` half was written by
walking the sessions actually opened, and the `refs` half was written by
guessing session numbers for the work that had not been opened — sessions 49-52
being roughly where the eye stopped.

⚠️ There is a trap in the file that would make that easy, and it is worth
knowing about independently of this defect: **`sessions` is not in id order.**
Reading down the list the ids go 58, 57, **64**, 56, 55, 54 — session 64 sits
between 57 and 56, and sessions 41, 43, 42, 40 are similarly out of order lower
down. Counting positions rather than reading ids gives the wrong number, and the
ids near 50 are exactly where the mis-numbering lands.

The cheap repair is to correct the four cards' `refs` from the table above and
add the matching `shipped` entries to sessions 23-28, 31, 35, 37, 38, 40 and 41
— which would also finish the job F-093 says was the point. The cheaper-looking
repair, deleting the refs, would satisfy the pin's first case and is why the pin
carries a second one asserting the links exist at all.

The R-239 half is one line: its third `verified_by` entry becomes
`src/test/auditAccess.test.tsx` with the case names the promotion gave them.
⚠️ Worth deciding rather than assumed: whether `render-plan.mjs --check` should
make the same demand of `verified_by.file` that it already makes of a defect's
`pin`. It would have caught this in the commit that caused it, and the seven
placeholder rows are the reason it probably was not written that way — they
would need a shape the validator can recognise as "not a path".

Whether S29 should exist beside S21/S22, or whether R-306/R-307 belong on S22,
is a judgement about the shape of the work and is the developer's; this defect
only says the record currently gives two answers.
