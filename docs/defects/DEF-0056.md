## Reproduction

`npx vitest run src/test/defects/DEF-0056.test.ts`

The pin is DEF-0041's, with other sentences: `groundReading` with the bar's own verb lists, asked
whether the heard text grounds the model's sweeping reading (an unassign of everyone with no place
-- a clear of the whole board).

## Expected

DEF-0041's expected, unchanged: "the sweeping reading must never reach a 'Do all' button unless the
person actually said a removal." A sentence that says not to clear the board, a question, or an
idiom is not a person saying to clear the board. `grounded.ts`'s own header for the rule: "Refusing
more is the safe side: a refusal only asks the person to say it again."

## Actual

Ten of ten ground the clear (each `expected { ok: true } to deeply equal { ok: false, reason: 'sweeping', ... }`):

```
GROUNDS  do not clear the board
GROUNDS  I did not say clear the board
GROUNDS  never clear the board
GROUNDS  don't clear everyone
GROUNDS  is everyone off today
GROUNDS  should I clear the board
GROUNDS  did we get everyone out
GROUNDS  I will clear it with everyone
GROUNDS  let me clear that with everybody tomorrow
GROUNDS  I would like to take everyone out tonight
```

The rule does refuse DEF-0041's own sentences, the reviewer's ("clear skies today", "everyone is out
to lunch"), and the tester's "who cleared everyone", "wipe the board clean" and "everyone off the
clock at five". Not checked: whether the served model produces the sweeping reading for any of the
ten (it was not running); DEF-0041's note shows it does produce sweeping readings from unrelated
speech.

## Lead

**A suspicion, not a fact.** `hasRemovalPhrase` matches the phrase's own words and what follows it
(`endsClause`), never what comes before it or between its parts. Three gaps: (1) nothing looks at
the words BEFORE the verb, so "do not", "don't", "never", "did not say", "should I" do not stop it;
(2) shape 1 lets two words sit between the verb and "everyone" (`SWEEP_VERB_REACH = 3`), which
admits "clear IT WITH everyone"; requiring the people word directly after the verb, or allowing only
"out"/"off"/"away" between, would close it; (3) shape 3 ("everyone off") and shape 2 ("take everyone
out") accept a leading "is"/"did we" and an idiom's "tonight". A negation or a question word in the
clause could simply refuse.
